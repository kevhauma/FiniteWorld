# Finite World — Minecraft worldgen mod design

Nov 19, 2026 · @Kevin

## Summary

Finite World is a Fabric mod that adds a world type with one landmass of a chosen radius inside an endless vanilla ocean. Every vanilla Overworld land biome and land structure is guaranteed to appear on that landmass at least once.

The land is generated by vanilla's own terrain and biome noise, at vanilla scale, and differs per seed. The mod changes two things on top of it: past the radius, land is pushed below sea level, and inside it a small number of biome *anchors* make sure nothing on the checklist is missing. Caves, cave biomes and underground structures keep generating everywhere, including under the ocean.

## Goals and non-goals

**Goals**

- No new land past the chosen radius. Ocean, caves and underground features continue without limit.
- Every vanilla Overworld land biome, cave biome and land or underground structure appears inside the radius at least once.
- The land looks and plays like vanilla: vanilla-scale biomes, natural shapes, a different layout per seed.
- The player picks the radius per world when creating it.

**Non-goals**

- Modded biomes and structures from other mods.
- Changes to the Nether or the End.
- Changing the radius after a world is created.
- Converting existing worlds.
- New in-game tools for checking the guarantee. Vanilla `/locate` and the Adventuring Time advancement already cover it.

## Player experience

The player picks **Finite** as the world type on the Create World screen and sets a radius. Normal worlds are not affected.

| Setting | Value |
| --- | --- |
| World type | Finite, added as a vanilla world preset |
| Radius field | Shown when Finite is selected |
| Default radius | 4,000 blocks |
| Minimum radius | About 3,000 blocks, enforced by the field |
| After creation | Radius is stored with the world and cannot change |

The minimum exists for two reasons. Below about 3,000 blocks, vanilla-scale biomes no longer fit reliably, so the guarantee breaks. A radius of 3,000 or more also keeps vanilla's first stronghold ring (1,280–2,816 blocks from the origin) on land.

The player spawns near the centre of the land. Walking outward, they cross vanilla land until the terrain runs into the sea, either at a beach or at a sea cliff (see The edge). Past that there is only ocean, but caves and underground structures continue below the sea floor.

## Land generation

Vanilla generates most of the land. Anchors fill in whatever the seed would otherwise leave out. In vanilla, terrain and biomes come from the same climate noises (temperature, humidity, continentalness, erosion, weirdness), so steering those noises in a region changes the biome and the terrain together.

**Anchors**

1. When the world loads, the anchor planner builds the checklist from vanilla's Overworld biome list, without the ocean biomes.
2. For each biome it searches inside the radius for the spot whose vanilla climate is already closest to that biome, keeping a minimum distance between anchors and from the edge band.
3. Around each spot it adds a smooth climate offset that pulls the area into that biome's climate range. The offset fades out with distance so the anchor blends into the land around it.
4. Placement is deterministic from the seed and radius, so anchors never need to be saved separately.

Biomes the seed already produces naturally still get an anchor; it just needs almost no nudge. Biomes can repeat freely; the guarantee is only a minimum.

**Checklist**

| Group | What must appear inside the radius |
| --- | --- |
| Surface biomes | Every vanilla Overworld land biome, including rivers, beaches and stony shore. Mushroom fields included. |
| Cave biomes | Lush caves, dripstone caves, deep dark (and any newer cave biome in the registry, such as sulfur caves in 26.3) |
| Structures | Each village type (plains, desert, savanna, snowy, taiga), pillager outpost, woodland mansion, desert pyramid, jungle pyramid, swamp hut, igloo, ruined portal, trail ruins, buried treasure, mineshaft (both variants), stronghold, trial chambers, ancient city |
| Ocean items | Not required. The outer ocean covers ocean biomes, monuments, ocean ruins and shipwrecks. They may still appear inside the radius. |

Structures keep vanilla's normal frequency. A required structure that doesn't form naturally is placed at a valid spot, normally inside its host biome's anchor: the mansion in a dark forest, the ancient city under the deep dark.

## The edge

The edge follows the terrain: flat land runs out as a natural coastline, and mountainous land can end as a sea cliff. You notice the radius only on a map, never as a perfect circle.

- **Fade band.** Near the radius, continentalness is lowered toward ocean values, so vanilla's own terrain turns into shore, a continental shelf and then deep ocean.
- **Adaptive width.** The band narrows where the terrain is high and rugged (peaks, windswept hills, badlands plateaus) and widens where it is flat. A narrow band under mountains makes a steep drop into deep water. A wide band on lowland makes beaches and bays.
- **Irregular outline.** The band's inner edge is offset by low-frequency noise, so the coastline wanders instead of tracing the circle.
- **No world border.** The ocean continues without limit.

## Beyond the radius

Past the edge band, generation is vanilla ocean with no land above sea level. Vanilla's 3D noise still raises the odd islet at ocean-level continentalness, so past the radius the final density is also capped: nothing at or above sea level is solid. Below the sea floor, it stays vanilla.

- **Ocean:** all vanilla ocean biomes (warm, lukewarm, cold, frozen and their deep variants) with monuments, ocean ruins and shipwrecks. Land that vanilla would raise here (islands, mushroom fields) stays below sea level.
- **Underground:** caves, aquifers, ore, cave biomes and underground structures (mineshafts, trial chambers, ancient cities) keep generating under the ocean.
- **Strongholds:** vanilla rings stay as they are. The first ring is on land (radius of at least about 3,000), later rings sit under the sea floor and are still reachable by digging.
- **Nether:** unchanged. Nether portals far from the origin will link to the ocean in the Overworld, which is expected.

## Technical approach

The mod adds three inputs (the radius, the edge band, the anchor planner) and leaves terrain, biome selection and structure generation to vanilla code.

&#91;embedded content: generation pipeline · 3 mod parts feeding vanilla\]

Because terrain and biomes both read the same climate noises, steering those noises keeps the land and its biomes consistent without a separate biome map.

- **World preset.** A `finite` world preset plus a copy of the Overworld noise settings whose noise router points at the mod's density functions. This makes Finite appear on the Create World screen.
- **Edge density function.** A custom density function type, registered in code, that lowers continentalness inside the fade band and beyond the radius. It takes the radius and band settings, so they are saved with the world's generation settings.
- **Anchor density function.** A second custom type that adds the planner's smooth offsets to temperature, humidity, continentalness, erosion and weirdness.
- **Anchor plan (ANC-1).** Density functions never see the seed, so the plan is reached through compilation instead of a mixin. Each of the five climate nodes is a `finiteworld:anchor` that references one shared `finiteworld:anchor_plan` node, whose children are the vanilla climate functions without anchors (so the planner never reads its own output) and which carries the radius. `RandomState` compiles every density function with one `CompileContext` of its own, which seeds those children; the plan node computes the plan the first time it is compiled with a context and caches it by that context (weak, identity keys, thread-safe), so all five nodes share one plan per world and Default worlds, which never reference the node, never plan. Measured on the stub (5 parameters on a 64-block grid, 12,281 points at radius 4,000): 46–73 ms per world on a dedicated server and in tests, up to about 230 ms with 16 worlds planning in parallel.
- **Anchor planner (ANC-2).** The `finiteworld:anchor_plan` node also carries the band and two registry fields: `biomes` (the `minecraft:overworld` multi-noise parameter list) and `exclude` (`#minecraft:is_ocean`). Its codec resolves both from the registries at datapack load, so the checklist is built at runtime without a hardcoded list or global registry access: 47 biomes in 26.3, cave biomes (dripstone, lush, sulfur caves, deep dark) included. The planner (`AnchorPlanner`, plain Java) drops depth and offset (it steers the 2D climate, so a cave anchor fits the column below), treats a range edge at ±1 as open (vanilla still picks that box past it), and merges each biome's touching boxes (3,789 boxes down to 849). It samples the climate on a 64-block grid inside `radius - band`, scores each spot by the squared distance to the nearest box shrunk by a 0.05 margin plus its four neighbours' mean, places the hardest biome first at its best spot that keeps the spacing, refines on a 16-block grid around it, and records the offset that pulls the spot into the box. Anchors have a 192-block reach, centres at least 384 apart, and the whole anchor disc stays inside `radius - band` (fixed values until TUNE-1). Measured at radius 4,000 (9,401 grid points): 329 ms on a dedicated server creating a world in a fresh JVM (world ready in 4.3 s, as before), 96–106 ms per world in the game tests once warm, and up to about 1.2 s with 16 worlds planning in parallel in a cold JVM.
- **Biome source.** Vanilla's multi-noise biome source reads the steered climate, so a custom biome source should not be needed. Add one only if testing shows gaps.
- **Radius UI.** A customise screen for the Finite preset, using the same hook vanilla uses for Superflat and Single Biome, that writes the radius into the generator settings and enforces the minimum.
- **Structures.** Vanilla structure sets stay unchanged. Required structures get an extra fixed-position placement taken from the anchor plan, used only when vanilla placement would miss them.
- **Spawn.** Spawn is forced near the centre of the land, so the player never starts at sea. Vanilla's spawn search does this with the Finite noise settings' `spawn_target`, which adds a distance term (`finiteworld:spawn_distance`) so the search stays within about 300 blocks of the origin (SPAWN-1).

## Risks and open questions

The biggest risk is anchors that look out of place; most other risks are about tuning, not design.

- **Out-of-place anchors.** If no spot has a nearby climate, an anchor can read as an odd patch (a snowy area next to a desert). Mitigation: score candidate spots by climate distance and by their neighbours' climate, not just the centre.
- **Mushroom fields.** They only form at ocean-level continentalness, so their anchor will usually end up coastal or as a near-shore island inside the radius. Acceptable, but check that it reads as natural.
- **Deep dark and ancient city.** These need low erosion and enough depth, which means mountains above. The anchor has to steer surface and underground climate together.
- **Cliff look.** A narrow fade band under mountains may produce sheer walls or floating overhangs. It needs tuning on several seeds.
- **Anchor search cost.** Searching the climate across a 4,000-block disc at world load must stay fast. Sample on a coarse grid first, then refine.
- **Future Minecraft versions.** Reading the checklist from vanilla's registries at runtime picks up new biomes automatically, but each new biome raises the space the minimum radius has to hold.
- **Open:** minimum distance between anchors, the width range of the fade band, and the exact minimum radius. Settle these in playtesting.
